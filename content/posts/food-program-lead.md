## Problem
Food program operations can break down quickly without consistent inventory tracking, demand planning, and process handoffs.

## Context
The program served over 150 students with recurring procurement, storage, and distribution pressure. The goal was reliability under day-to-day operational constraints, not theoretical optimization.

## What I Built
I led logistics and operating workflows, introduced clearer inventory controls, and used lightweight analysis to improve planning and reduce avoidable waste.

## Key Decisions
- Created practical KPIs that operators could update and act on weekly.
- Used demand patterns to guide procurement and reorder timing.
- Standardized handoff steps so team execution was less person-dependent.

## Tradeoffs
- Chose simple forecasting approaches over complex models to improve adoption.
- Prioritized consistency and service reliability over aggressive cost-cutting.
- Focused on operational transparency before expanding scope.

## Impact
- Improved service reliability across recurring distribution cycles.
- Reduced inventory volatility through clearer planning cadence.
- Created reusable operating structure for future student teams.

## Tech Stack
`Operational Analytics` `Demand Forecasting` `KPI Design` `Process Documentation`

- `Operations`: Managed execution quality under real logistic constraints.
- `Consulting`: Turned messy workflow pain points into practical operating changes.
- `APM`: Coordinated stakeholders and translated goals into systems.
- `Data / Analytics`: Used data as a decision tool inside day-to-day operations.
